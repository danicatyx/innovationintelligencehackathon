01Thesis

Not another generic hackathon.

01

Build for a real problem.

Name a market and a customer, not just a demo. Sharpest insight beats largest codebase.

02

Think past 4:00 PM.

Judging weighs insight and venture potential — evidence of something worth continuing.

03

Work next to people who build and invest.

Founders, operators, and investors from SCET and DECODE are in the room all week.

04

Demo like it matters.

Three minutes, live, on October 11. A startup demo, not a class presentation.

02Process

How it works.

Four steps from a pain point to a live demo.

01

Choose a track

Start from a real problem in one of five areas.

02

Build your team

Form a team across engineering, design, research, and business.

03

Build for a week

Turn the idea into a working prototype.

04

Demo live

Present to founders, investors, and the Tech Week community.

Great companies often begin as a small experiment run by people who couldn't stop thinking about a problem.

A full week to build — long enough to talk to real users and ship something that works — and three hours on October 11 to prove it.

We are not looking for finished companies. We are looking for evidence of something worth continuing.

05What wins

What we're looking for.

Not a finished company. Evidence of something worth continuing.

01
A problem someone actually has

Named customer, named market. Not a use case invented to fit the tech.

02
An insight the room missed

Something you understood about the problem that others did not.

03
Software that runs

A working prototype beats a deck. Rough edges are fine; vapor is not.

04
A reason to keep going

A first customer, a next step, a team that wants to build it past Sunday.

Recognition
Best Overall

07Questions

Questions.

Who can apply?+

Undergrads, grad students, and recent graduates from any school — technical or not.

Do I need a team or an idea beforehand?+

No. Apply solo or with a team — teams form at the October 5 kickoff.

Can I participate if I'm not a strong programmer?+

Yes. The strongest teams mix technical, design, and market thinking.

Do teams get any support during the week?+

Yes. Build week runs like a short accelerator: daily office hours on campus and online, covering problem framing, technical review, and pitch practice.

Do I have to fit a track?+

No. Tracks carry their own prizes, but every project competes for the general awards.

Can I use existing code, and can I keep working after?+

Existing code, open-source, and AI tools are fine — the demo must be built Oct 5–10. You keep your IP.

How are projects judged?+

Five weighted dimensions — the full rubric is above.

Will the event be photographed?+

Yes. Photos and video use are covered in the participation terms; ask an organizer to be excluded from published media.

When do decisions go out?+

Decisions are sent by email on a rolling basis after the priority deadline. There is no status portal.

Participation terms

Terms of participation.

Innovation Intelligence Hackathon · Fall 2026

Submitting an application confirms your agreement to the terms below. They cover conduct, media, data, intellectual property, and eligibility for the build week of October 5–10 and Demo Day on October 11, 2026.

01

Conduct

All participants, mentors, judges, and organizers are expected to behave professionally toward one another. Harassment, discrimination, plagiarism, misrepresentation of work, and interference with another team are grounds for removal from the event at the organizers’ discretion. Concerns may be raised with any organizer during the event or submitted through the application form.

02

Photography and media

The event is photographed and recorded. Images, video, and audio captured during build week and Demo Day may be used in event recaps, program materials, and partner communications, and may include team names and project summaries. Participants who prefer not to appear in published media may notify an organizer at any time and will be excluded.

03

Data and privacy

Application responses are accessible only to the organizing team and are used solely to evaluate applications and operate the event. Data is not sold or shared with third parties for marketing purposes. Participants may request deletion of their application data after the event.

04

Intellectual property

Participants retain full ownership of all work created during the event. No rights are transferred to the organizers, host programs, or partners. Organizers may publicly reference a project by name with a brief summary as part of event coverage.

05

Eligibility and submissions

Participation is open to current students and recent graduates. Teams may include one to four members. Projects must be developed during the build week of October 5–10; pre-existing code, open-source libraries, and AI tools are permitted. Submissions close at 11:59 PM on October 10. Demo Day attendance is in person at UC Berkeley.

06

Changes

Schedule, format, judging panel, and awards are subject to change. Material changes will be communicated by email to accepted participants.
